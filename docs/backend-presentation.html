Architecture

One server behind five screens

3 / 10Python · FastAPI · scikit-learn · SQLite · Ollama

Five screens talk to one Python server. The server checks every request and keeps the steps in order. Behind it are two models we trained ourselves, plain rules, a small language model that runs on the same machine, and one database file. No outside AI service is called, so what a patient types stays on our server.

The chain

Every visit moves through five states

registeredthe phone gets a number and a questionnaire
nursecheck-in done, waits for the nurse
waitingthe nurse set a level, ticket in the line
with doctorcalled to a room
doneclosed, and kept

The server keeps the order

  • A step out of order is refused, not guessed.
  • Two clicks at once cannot send one patient to two doctors: the check and the change happen in one locked step.
  • Levels 1 and 2 never wait in the line.

Urgency is not decided once

  • "I feel worse" on the phone puts the patient on top of the nurse's list. It never moves them up by itself.
  • A waiting patient is due a second look after 30, 60 or 120 minutes, by level.
  • While the nurse re-checks, no doctor can call that patient away.

Where the AI is

Three AI parts, each with one narrow job

Trained by us

Urgency model

Reads the complaint in words, age, how the patient came, alertness, pain and six vital signs. Gives a probability for each of the five levels.

Logistic regression. It never sees the nurse's level, the diagnosis or the outcome.

Trained by us

Department model

Suggests one of nine departments, together with a key-word rule. The right one is among the four it offers 92% of the time.

Alone it is right 70%; the key-word rule 67%; always the biggest department 21%.

Steered, not trained

Language model

Words three history questions for the doctor and a two-sentence summary for the nurse.

Plain code throws away any sentence with a guess, advice, a diagnosis, or a symptom the patient never mentioned.

Safety

The rules are in the code, not in a promise

What the patient's phone can get

  • Its own ticket, place in line and room. Never the level, never another patient.
  • The doctor's note goes only to the phone that registered the visit.
  • A "yes" to a danger sign counts only for the server's own hand-written questions.

What Meddy may say

  • A level, a department, a question. Never a diagnosis.
  • "Not asked" is never shown as "no".
  • The nurse reads the AI's summary first and can hold it back from the doctor.

Who can open what

The nurse's, doctor's and chief doctor's screens sit behind a staff PIN: they show names, and their buttons can close visits. The waiting-room board is open and shows tickets and rooms only.

7 / 10Each rule has a test that fails if someone breaks it

The safety rules are written in code. The patient's phone gets its own ticket and place, never the urgency level and never another patient. Meddy can suggest a level and a department, never a diagnosis. And "not asked" is never shown as "no". Each of these rules has a test that fails if someone breaks it.

Memory

One database file that keeps everything

Seven tables

  • visits: every visit from the door to the doctor
  • answers: what the patient tapped and wrote
  • events: a log of every step, with its time and who did it
  • doctors and departments: who is on duty where
  • people: one open visit per insurance card
  • meta: the clock and the counters

What it promises

  • A restart or a new version loses nothing: an older file is upgraded in place.
  • "Start a new day" closes visits and keeps them.
  • The insurance card number is never stored: only a one-way fingerprint and its last four characters.
  • Insurance never reaches a model and never changes the level.

Everything is kept in one database file with seven tables. Every step of every visit is logged with its time and who did it. A restart or a new version loses nothing. And we never store an insurance card number, only a one-way fingerprint, so one person can have one open visit at a time.

Limits

Where it fails, and what comes next

It fails here
  • It misses urgent patients whose danger does not show in the measurements: 19 were given a low level, and nurses rated 14 of those correctly.
  • It does not travel: trained at one hospital and tested at the other, 68% drops to 55–58%.
  • The data was written by nurses, not typed by patients. Waiting times are simulated.
Next
  • Try the flags and the report with real triage nurses.
  • Collect complaints written by patients themselves.
  • Train and test on more hospitals.
